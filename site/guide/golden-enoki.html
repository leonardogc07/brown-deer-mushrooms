Golden Enoki

Golden enoki is a bundle of long, thin stems and tiny caps. The taste is mild, and the stems stay a little crisp if we cook them briefly. More ideas from other cooks are on our golden enoki recipes page.

Meet the Mushroom

Raw, the flavor is light and a little sweet. Warming it brings out a mild savory taste, less earthy than a cremini.

The cluster is tied together at a spongy base. We trim that base off, then pull the stems apart so they cook evenly.

A short cook keeps the snap in the stems. Leave them in a simmering pot for a long time and they turn soft and limp. We add them near the end.

How We Grow It

We grow golden enoki on the farm in Winston-Salem and harvest the clusters by hand. Golden enoki like cooler temperatures. We colonize the blocks at or below 70°F for about 60 days, then fruit them below 65°F at about 85% RH for about 2 weeks.

Storage

Keep the cluster dry and cold. A paper bag in the refrigerator lets it breathe. Cook it while the stems are still crisp and the caps are still small and dry.

They keep in the fridge for about 7 to 10 days.

If the stems turn slimy or the smell goes sour, compost the cluster. Do not hide that in a soup.

How to Cook It

  1. Trim off the spongy base where the stems are bundled. Separate the stems with your hands. Skip a soak. Wet stems steam instead of staying crisp.
  2. For soup or hot pot, drop the stems in near the end. They are ready when they soften and still have a little bite.
  3. For a stir-fry, add them last over high heat with a pinch of salt. Toss once or twice. Pull them when the stems are glossy and still a bit snappy.

Recipes

Miso soup

Simmer the broth, then add the trimmed stems and take the pot off the heat once they soften. Stir in miso off the boil so it stays smooth. The written recipe is miso soup with enoki and ground sesame on our recipes list.

Hot pot

Set a cluster beside the other ingredients and swish the stems through the simmering broth for a short minute. They pick up the broth and stay distinct from the greens and tofu.

Quick stir-fry

Cook garlic or scallions in a little oil, add the separated stems at the end, and toss with salt. They are done when they glisten and still bend with a snap. We put them next to rice or noodles.

Grow It at Home

Growing difficulty

  • Medium to hard

We rate growing golden enoki at home as medium to hard. The long colonization and fruiting times give a block more time to dry out or get contaminated.

When a home grow is ready, cook it the same way we cook the mushrooms from the farm. If you would rather buy fresh mushrooms, ask us what we have this week.
