Which of our mushrooms should you cook?

We grow lion's mane, oyster, chestnut, and golden enoki on our farm in Winston-Salem. This page puts the notes from each guide in one place so we can pick a mushroom for the pan we have in mind.

Mushroom Taste Texture How we cook it Fridge life Grow at home
Lion's mane Mild and a little sweet raw. After a hard sear it tastes savory. We mostly taste browned mushroom and butter. Soft teeth on the outside. Dense flesh that shreds into strands. Hard sear, as a steak or shredded into a pan. A boil makes it soft and watery. About two more weeks in a kraft paper bag, while it is still white and springy. Easy with a kit. Harder from scratch.
Oyster Mild and a little sweet raw. A hot pan makes them savory. Some clusters smell faintly of anise before they cook. Soft, fan-shaped caps in a cluster. They cook down fast. Hot pan in a single layer so the edges brown. A crowded, wet pan steams them. Large caps can be battered and fried. About two weeks in a paper bag, while the caps are still firm. Easy with a kit. Harder from scratch.
Chestnut Mild raw. A hot pan brings out a nutty, savory taste. Small rounded caps on short stems. They stay firm if you do not crowd them, and they release some water as they cook. Sear or roast hot, mostly whole. Let the water cook off before you add a sauce. We also pickle them. About 1 week in a paper bag, while the caps are still firm. Hard.
Golden enoki Mild. Raw, it is light and a little sweet. Warming it brings a mild savory taste, less earthy than a cremini. Long thin stems and tiny caps. A short cook keeps a little snap. A long simmer turns them soft and limp. Soup, hot pot, or a quick stir-fry. Add the stems near the end. About 7 to 10 days in a paper bag, while the stems are still crisp. Medium to hard.

Lion's mane

We cook lion's mane when we want a mushroom that can sit in the middle of the plate. Raw, it is mild and faintly sweet. A hard sear browns it, and the dense flesh shreds into strands, which is what makes a steak or a patty work. We skip a boil. Fresh Brown Deer lion's mane keeps in the fridge for about two more weeks in a kraft paper bag. A fruiting kit is the easy start at home. Spawn or a log is a harder project.

Oyster

Oyster mushrooms cook quickly in a pan. Raw, they are mild and a little sweet, and a hot pan makes them savory. The caps are soft and fan-shaped, and they cook down fast, so we give them room if we want the edges to brown. Our blue oysters keep about two weeks in the refrigerator in a paper bag. A kit is easy if you check it each day. Starting from spawn is harder.

Chestnut

Chestnut mushrooms hold their shape in a hot pan and taste a little nutty once the caps brown. The caps are small and rounded. They release some water, so we let that cook off before a sauce. They keep well in the fridge for about 1 week in a paper bag. We rate growing chestnut at home as hard, because the long colonization and fruiting times make a block more likely to dry out or get contaminated.

Golden enoki

Golden enoki is a bundle of long, thin stems and tiny caps. The taste is mild, and the stems stay a little crisp if we cook them briefly in soup, hot pot, or a stir-fry. They keep in the fridge for about 7 to 10 days. We rate growing golden enoki at home as medium to hard.
